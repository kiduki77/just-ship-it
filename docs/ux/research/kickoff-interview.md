# Kickoff interview research notes

Goal (restated). The owner of Just Ship It wants the CEO agent to interview them before planning, so the roadmap is built from what the owner actually knows, wants, and fears instead of from a name and a one-line goal. Questions must be plain enough for a grade-schooler and must not overwhelm.

Labels used below.
- **Verified** means I read the cited page this session and it says this.
- **Secondary** means a third-party summary I read, not the primary source.
- **Unverified** means I could not confirm the wording or did not reach a source.
- **Design inference** means my own synthesis, not a claim from a source.

## Part A. What the sources say

### A1. Y Combinator application and partner questions

- YC's "How to apply" page asks "What is your company going to make?" and advises a plain, concrete first sentence, no marketing language, and the test "could a reader rebuild your product from it?". It also says to disclose known flaws, because reviewers assume any problem you don't mention is one you haven't considered. Verified. https://www.ycombinator.com/howtoapply.html
- The same page asks founders for "something impressive that each founder has built or achieved" and the "hacked some (non-computer) system" question. Verified. https://www.ycombinator.com/howtoapply.html
- A 2023 breakdown of the application quotes the Idea section: "Why did you pick this idea to work on?", "Do you have domain expertise in this area?", "How do you know people need what you're making?", "Who are your competitors?", "What do you understand about your business that they don't?", "How do or will you make money? How much could you make?", "How do users find your product?". Progress: "How far along are you?", "How long have each of you been working on this? How much of that has been full-time?". Equity: "How much money do you spend per month?", "How long is your runway?". It also has "If you are applying with the same idea as a previous batch, did anything change?" and "If you applied with a different idea, why did you pivot and what did you learn from the last idea?". Secondary (the post says questions change by batch). https://nickraushenbush.substack.com/p/y-combinator-application-breakdown
- The current live form is at apply.ycombinator.com and was not readable. Unverified for the current batch wording. https://www.ycombinator.com/apply
- Michael Seibel office hours: "How much time have you been working and what have you done?" and the goal check "Where do you want to be week over week to get to where you want to go?". He wants a company explained quickly, with facts, and without jargon. Verified (summary of an episode page). https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/office-hours-with-michael-seibel
- Seibel on interviews: "a minute into the interview, we don't know what they do" is the number one problem. Secondary (Quartz). https://qz.com/africa/795674/how-to-get-into-y-combinator-ceo-michael-seibels-advice-to-nigerian-start-up-founders
- YC "How to Talk to Users" (Eric Migicovsky) has a chapter "What's the hardest part about doing the thing you're trying to solve?". Verified as a chapter title. https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/startup-school-week-1-recap-kevin-hale-and-eric-migicovsky
- The rest of Migicovsky's five questions ("Tell me about the last time...", "Why was that hard?", "What, if anything, have you done to solve it?", "What don't you love about the solutions you've tried?") are Unverified. Only a vendor summary describes the chain, general to specific. Secondary. https://www.koji.so/docs/how-to-talk-to-users-yc-method
- Migicovsky's own blog says the talk was "based off the Mom Test". Verified. https://ericmigi.com/blog/startup-school-how-to-talk-to-users

### A2. Paul Graham and Sam Altman

- Paul Graham, "How to Get Startup Ideas": ask "who wants this right now?" and whether people would use it "even when it's a crappy version one". Prefer a deep narrow "well" over a shallow hole, a small market with "a fast path out of" it, and problems "you have yourself". Ideas should "grow naturally out of the founders' own experiences". "Live in the future, then build what's missing." Verified. https://paulgraham.com/startupideas.html
- Sam Altman, Startup Playbook: "We ask how big it is today, how fast it's growing, and why it's going to be big in ten years." "So we ask why founders want to start this particular company." "We look for clear, concise answers here." "complex ideas are almost always a sign of muddled thinking or a made up problem." "The way to test an idea is to either launch it and see what happens or try to sell it." "It's much better to first make a product a small number of users love than a product that a large number of users like." Verified. https://playbook.samaltman.com/

### A3. Lean Canvas (Ash Maurya)

- The current Lean Canvas page (redirected from leancanvas.com) lists blocks including Customer Segments, Problem (top 3), Existing Alternatives, Unique Value Proposition, Solution, Key Metrics, Channels, Early Adopters, Cost Structure, Revenue Streams, Unfair Advantage, High-Level Concept ("X for Y"). Verified (the page shows 12 named items, the classic canvas has 9 boxes with sub-items). https://leanspark.ai/leancanvas
- Fill order: start with the problem, then customer segments and value proposition, then solution and unfair advantage, then revenue and cost, then key metrics and channels. Maurya also says on order, "There isn't one." Secondary (Lucid). https://app.lucid.co/blog/lean-canvas-model
- The page says early-stage ventures need to understand their problem deeply before worrying about key partners. Verified. https://leanspark.ai/leancanvas

### A4. Amazon Working Backwards (PR/FAQ)

- The five customer questions, as given in an AWS re:Invent 2019 deck: Who is the customer? What is the customer problem or opportunity? What is the most important customer benefit? How do you know what your customer needs or wants? What does the customer experience look like? Secondary (search snippet of the AWS deck, I did not open the PDF). https://d1.awsstatic.com/events/reinvent/2019/REPEAT_1_Working_backwards_Amazon%E2%80%99s_approach_to_innovation_ENT207-R1.pdf
- Commoncog notes that early on it is often unclear who the customer is, and initial ideas about the customer are often mistaken. Secondary (snippet). https://commoncog.com/putting-amazons-pr-faq-to-practice/
- Reviewer checklist: "Is the customer clearly defined?", "Is the problem clearly defined?", "Which dimension(s) is the new product better, cheaper, or faster than substitutes?", "Would we expect customers to change their behavior to buy/use the solution?". Internal FAQ topics include current alternatives, regulatory or legal issues, upfront investment, "Assumptions that must hold for success", "Top three reasons the product could fail". Verified. https://www.workingbackwards.com/concepts/working-backwards-pr-faq-process

### A5. Basecamp Shape Up

- Pitch ingredients: Problem ("The raw idea, a use case, or something we've seen that motivates us to work on this", best as one specific story), Appetite ("The amount of time we want to spend on a project, as opposed to an estimate"), Solution, Rabbit holes ("Part of a project that is too unknown, complex, or open-ended to bet on"), No-gos (features or use cases deliberately excluded). Verified. https://basecamp.com/shapeup/1.5-chapter-06
- "Fixed time, variable scope" is key. Small batch is 1 to 2 week projects, big batch is a whole six-week cycle. Ask "But what is driving the request?" and shift "from asking 'What could we build?' to 'What's really going wrong?'". Verified. https://basecamp.com/shapeup/1.2-chapter-03

### A6. Jobs To Be Done switch interview

- The switch interview walks "backward through their decision in forensic detail" and reveals "the real timeline of the purchase", including the first thought, events that pushed the person forward, anxieties that held them back, and the moment they decided. Verified. https://jobstobedone.org/learn/
- Four forces: "the push of the current situation", "the pull of the new solution", "the anxiety of the unknown", "the habit of the present". "A switch only happens when push and pull are strong enough to overcome anxiety and habit." Verified. https://jobstobedone.org/learn/
- Named timeline stages (passive looking, active looking, deciding, consuming) are Unverified. I did not reach the Timeline page.

### A7. The Mom Test (Rob Fitzpatrick)

- Talk about their life, not your idea. Ask about past specifics, not future opinions. Validate by asking for commitments. Verified (Sachin Rekhi summary, Secondary to the book). https://www.sachinrekhi.com/the-mom-test-rob-fitzpatrick
- Bad: "do you think it's a good product idea?", "would you buy a product which did X?", pitching and asking "do you like it?". Good: "What are your big goals and focuses right now?", how they solve it today and what it costs in money and time, "Why not?", "When's the last time you did try?". Compliments such as "I would definitely buy that" are cheap and can't be trusted. Commitments cost time, reputation, or money (next meeting, introduction, pre-order). "it's not a real sales lead until you've given them such a concrete chance to reject you." Secondary. https://www.sachinrekhi.com/the-mom-test-rob-fitzpatrick
- "Sometimes" could mean once a year or once a day, so vague frequency needs a follow-up. Secondary (search snippet). https://www.koji.so/docs/mom-test-methodology

### A8. Kickoff and charter templates

- Atlassian Project Kickoff: 90 minutes. Roles (sponsor, leader, facilitator, core team, stakeholders). Vision prompt "Why are you pursuing this project?". Mission prompts, what are you trying to do and how will you achieve the vision. Mission tests prompt, what will success look like when the project is complete. Follow-up: OKRs, a DACI, timeline and milestones, "Revisit the plans and project poster as the project progresses". Verified. https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/project-kickoff
- Atlassian Project Poster: Problem space ("Explain why solving this problem matters to your customers and to the business"), Validation ("knowledge gaps and risks", defining the problem space "will force you to make assumptions that need to be validated"), Ready to make it. Verified. https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/project-poster
- Atlassian DACI: Driver, Approver ("the one person (yes: one!) who makes the decision"), Contributors ("a voice, but not a vote"), Informed ("no vote, no voice"). Document the decision so future teams understand how and why. Verified. https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/daci
- Marty Cagan opportunity assessment: "Exactly what problem will this solve?", "For whom do we solve that problem?", "How big is the opportunity?", "What alternatives are out there?", "Why are we best suited to pursue this?", "Why now?", "How will we get this product to market?", "How will we measure success/make money from this product?", "What factors are critical to success?", "what's the recommendation? (go or no-go)". Verified. https://www.svpg.com/assessing-product-opportunities/
- GV Design Sprint Monday: "start at the end" with a long-term goal, map the challenge, ask the experts at your company, pick a target "an ambitious but manageable piece of the problem". Verified. https://www.gv.com/sprint/
- Sprint questions framed as "To meet our long-term goal, what has to be true?" plus imagining the project failed, and the goal horizon of six months, a year, or five years. Secondary (summaries). https://www.nirandfar.com/good-products-start-good-questions/ and https://paulminors.com/blog/sprint-jake-knapp-book-summary-pdf/
- Google Design Sprint Kit "Understand" page did not render. Unverified. https://designsprintkit.withgoogle.com/methodology/phase1-understand
- IDEO discovery methods. Not researched this session. Unverified.

### A9. Risk, assumptions, failure

- Gary Klein premortem (HBR 2007, "Performing a Project Premortem"): assume the project has already failed and list plausible reasons why, run at the start. Secondary (Wikipedia and summaries). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-mortem
- Imagining the event already happened raised the number of reasons generated by about 30 percent. Secondary and contested (more reasons, not necessarily more correct ones). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-mortem
- Assumptions mapping (David Bland, Strategyzer): write hypotheses as "We believe that...", tag desirability, viability, feasibility (and adaptability), plot on importance versus evidence, and test the important, no-evidence quadrant first ("You'll want your teams to focus their near term experimentation here"). Verified. https://strategyzer.com/library/how-assumptions-mapping-can-focus-your-teams-on-running-experiments-that-matter

### A10. How skilled interviewers handle non-experts

- Playback: "Rephrase what you heard and ask 'Did I get that right?' Misunderstandings caught mid-interview save weeks of rework." Send a written summary within 24 hours. Secondary (practitioner guide). https://sharedcontext.ai/skills/external/The-AI-Directory-Company/stakeholder-interview
- "Summarize key points back to confirm understanding before closing." Secondary (University of Arkansas discovery guide). https://uark.teamdynamix.com/TDClient/33/Portal/KB/PrintArticle?ID=1539
- Notes should "reflect the client's words, not the consultant's interpretation". Secondary. https://www.meetjamie.ai/templates/discovery-session
- Open with the project summary and ask "Does this match your understanding, or would you frame it differently?". Ask who else has relevant information. Secondary. https://sharedcontext.ai/skills/external/The-AI-Directory-Company/stakeholder-interview
- Motivational interviewing OARS (open questions, affirmations, reflections, summaries). Open questions start with How, What, "tell me more". Don't ask three questions in a row. Summaries work well at transition points. Secondary (training handouts citing Miller and Rollnick). https://www.issup.net/node/5871 and https://www.aipc.net.au/articles/motivational-interviewing-techniques/
- NN/g: "What users say and what they do are different." Leading or pressing makes people "make up opinions". Asked how they usually do a task, people "describe an idealized workflow". Ask them to "recall specific instances". "People can make up an opinion about anything, and they'll do so if asked." Verified. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/interviewing-users/

### A11. Length and "I don't know"

- SurveyMonkey (about 100,000 surveys, 2009 to 2010): more questions means more drop-off, the sharpest drop is in the first 15 questions. Verified via search summary of the SurveyMonkey page, old data. https://www.surveymonkey.com/curiosity/survey_questions_and_completion_rates/
- Qualtrics: think in minutes, not question count. Drastic drop-off beyond about 12 minutes on desktop and 9 on mobile. Completion declines with more than three open-text questions. Secondary (search summary). https://www.qualtrics.com/blog/4-tips-for-preventing-drop-offs-in-surveys/
- "Don't know" options: the satisficing view (Krosnick et al. 2002) says they can invite low effort. Dolnicar and Grun (2014) found "Don't know" is mostly used when respondents genuinely cannot answer. Sturgis, Roberts and Smith found many midpoint answers are disguised non-opinions. Secondary (abstracts). https://www.scinapse.io/papers/2014097054 and https://study.sagepub.com/sites/default/files/sturgisrobertssmith.pdf

## Part B. Design inputs

Everything in Part B is design inference built on Part A unless a citation says otherwise. These are survey and research-interview findings applied to a one-person owner interview, which is a different setting.

### B1. Question set

Rules for every question (from A1, A2, A7, A10):
- One question per turn, an everyday word in place of every term (no TAM, MVP, KPI, OKR).
- Ask about real past events and things the owner has seen, not opinions about the future.
- Every question has three escape hatches: example answers to pick from, "잘 모르겠어요", and "나중에".
- Each group ends with a playback.

Required for the first roadmap: G0, G1, G2, G4, G5, G7. G3 and G6 run short, with defaults. The fill-later items are listed at the end.

**G0. 한 문장 (warm-up)**
1. "이 프로젝트를 친구에게 한 문장으로 설명한다면 뭐라고 할 거예요?" (YC "What is your company going to make?", Lean Canvas High-Level Concept "X for Y", Altman "clear, concise answers")
   Example chips: "OO를 위한 OO", "OO 같은데 OO용".
2. "이게 다 만들어지면, 누가 무엇을 할 수 있게 돼요?" (Amazon "What does the customer experience look like?")

What a good answer unlocks: a project title and a test sentence for every later stage ("does this task serve the one sentence?"). If the answer needs a paragraph, Altman's "muddled thinking" warning applies, so G2 needs more time.

**G1. 왜 (why and motivation)**
1. "이 생각이 처음 든 건 언제, 무슨 일 때문이었어요?" (JTBD "first thought", PG "grow naturally out of the founders' own experiences", YC "Why did you pick this idea to work on?")
2. "이게 잘 되면, 당신에게는 뭐가 달라져요? 돈, 시간, 재미, 자랑, 다른 사람 돕기 중 무엇이 제일 커요?" (Altman "why founders want to start this particular company", Atlassian vision "Why are you pursuing this project?")
3. "왜 하필 지금이에요?" (Cagan "Why now?", Altman "major technological shifts are just starting")

Unlocks: the tiebreaker the CEO uses when tradeoffs collide. A "money" project and a "learning" project get different roadmaps. The "why now" answer sets urgency.

**G2. 누구의 어떤 문제 (customer and problem)**
1. "이걸 제일 필요로 하는 사람 한 명을 떠올려 보세요. 어떤 사람이에요?" (Amazon "Who is the customer?", Lean Canvas Early Adopters, PG "who wants this right now?")
2. "그 사람이 그 문제 때문에 곤란했던 일, 실제로 본 적 있어요? 마지막으로 본 건 언제예요?" (Mom Test past specifics, NN/g "recall specific instances", Shape Up problem as one specific story)
3. "그 사람은 지금 그 문제를 어떻게 해결하고 있어요? 돈이나 시간이 얼마나 들어요?" (Lean Canvas Existing Alternatives, Mom Test cost in money and time, Cagan "What alternatives are out there?")
4. "그 사람이 정말 원한다는 걸 어떻게 알아요? 들은 말인지, 본 행동인지, 짐작인지 골라 주세요." (YC "How do you know people need what you're making?", Amazon "How do you know what your customer needs or wants?")

Unlocks: the first target segment and whether stage 1 is "go talk to customers" or can skip ahead. The Q4 answer type (heard, seen, guess) sets the evidence level on the assumption map directly.

**G3. 이미 가진 것 (assets, skills, network, data)**
1. "이 일과 관련해서 이미 잘하는 것, 해본 것이 있어요?" (YC "Do you have domain expertise in this area?", "something impressive")
2. "도와줄 수 있는 사람, 이미 아는 손님, 모아 둔 자료나 계정이 있어요?" (Lean Canvas Unfair Advantage and Channels, GV "ask the experts", stakeholder guide "who else might have valuable information")
3. "이미 만들어 두었거나 해본 게 있어요? 해봤는데 안 된 것도 좋아요." (YC "How far along are you?", "why did you pivot and what did you learn")

Unlocks: which stages to skip, the first channel (existing contacts), which agent tasks need owner-provided material, and failed attempts the roadmap must not repeat.

**G4. 지켜야 할 선 (constraints)**
1. "이 프로젝트에 한 달에 최대 얼마까지 써도 괜찮아요?" Chips: 0원, 5만원 이하, 50만원 이하, 그 이상. (YC "How much money do you spend per month?", "How long is your runway?")
2. "얼마 동안 해보고 판단하고 싶어요? 2주, 6주, 3개월?" (Shape Up appetite, "fixed time, variable scope")
3. "법이나 규칙 때문에 조심해야 할 게 있을까요? 예를 들어 개인정보, 의료, 돈, 아이들." (Amazon internal FAQ "Regulatory or legal issues")
4. "절대 하면 안 되는 일이 있다면요? 예를 들어 내 이름으로 글 올리기, 손님에게 직접 연락, 유료 광고." (Shape Up No-gos)

Unlocks: the budget gate and spend caps, the appetite that sizes every stage, legal tasks that come first, and red lines the CEO turns into hard policy (connector rules, Approval Center triggers).

**G5. 성공과 실패**
1. "정한 기간이 끝났을 때 '잘 됐다!'라고 말하려면 무엇을 보면 돼요? 숫자 하나로요." Chips: 첫 손님 1명, 사용자 10명, 매출 OO원, 대기 명단 OO명. (Atlassian mission tests, Sprint long-term goal, Cagan "How will we measure success", Lean Canvas Key Metrics, Altman "a small number of users love")
2. "기간이 끝났는데 실패했다고 상상해 봐요. 무엇 때문일 것 같아요?" (Klein premortem, Amazon "Top three reasons the product could fail", Sprint "imagine the project failed")
3. "어떤 일이 생기면 그만두거나 방향을 바꿀 거예요?" (Cagan "go or no-go")

Unlocks: the gate criteria, so every stage gate has a numeric pass line and a stop line. The premortem answers become the first risk list and the first rows of the assumption map.

**G6. 속도와 위험 감수**
1. "어느 쪽이 더 좋아요? (가) 작게 빨리 내보내고 고치기, (나) 제대로 준비해서 한 번에." (Shape Up small vs big batch, Altman "launch it and see what happens or try to sell it")
2. "돈이나 시간을 잃어도 괜찮은 정도는 어디까지예요?" (design inference, ties to G4 Q1)

Unlocks: stage granularity (many small gates or few big ones) and how much the CEO may spend on experiments before asking. Default when skipped: small and fast, which matches Altman and Shape Up. No source in this research measures founder risk appetite directly. Unverified as a framework.

**G7. 직접 정할 것과 맡길 것**
1. "이 프로젝트에서 꼭 당신이 직접 정하고 싶은 것 세 가지는요?" Chips: 가격, 이름과 디자인, 돈 쓰기, 외부에 내보내는 글. (DACI Approver)
2. "알아서 해도 되고 나중에 알려주기만 하면 되는 건요?" (DACI Informed)
3. "의견만 주고 싶은 건요?" (DACI Contributors, "a voice, but not a vote")

Unlocks: the Approval Center policy for this project. Approver items always pause, Informed items go to the activity log, Contributor items get a comment window.

**Fill-later items (default to research tasks):** competitors and "what you know that they don't" (YC), revenue model and price (YC, Lean Canvas Revenue Streams), market size and growth (Altman, Cagan), channels beyond existing contacts, cost structure, unfair advantage. Lean Canvas puts channels and metrics last and Maurya says order is flexible (Lucid, Secondary), which supports deferring them.

Count: 22 core questions, about 15 required. That sits near the SurveyMonkey 15-question drop-off point, so the required set should be the first sitting.

### B2. How the interview runs

1. **Open with the frame.** "10분쯤 걸려요. 모르면 '모르겠어요'라고 해도 돼요. 제가 대신 알아볼게요. 나중에 언제든 고칠 수 있어요." This is permission to not know, which counters the NN/g "make up an opinion" effect.
2. **Seed from what exists.** Prefill from the project name and one-line goal and open G0 with a playback ("지금 제가 아는 건 이거예요. 맞나요, 다르게 말하고 싶나요?"), the stakeholder-guide opener.
3. **One question per turn, free text first, then chips.** Show example chips only after a pause, an "잘 모르겠어요", or a very short answer. Chips are examples, never the only options, and always include "다른 거예요" and "모르겠어요". Showing chips first leads the answer (NN/g on leading).
4. **Adaptive follow-ups (max 2 per question)** decided by answer type:
   - Vague frequency or "가끔" → "지난주에는요? 지난달에는요?" (Mom Test "sometimes" problem)
   - Opinion or future ("사람들이 좋아할 거예요") → "그렇게 생각하게 된 일이 있었어요?" (Mom Test past specifics)
   - Compliment-type evidence ("친구가 좋대요") → "그 친구가 돈이나 시간을 쓰겠다고 했어요?" (Mom Test commitments)
   - Big or abstract answer → "그중에서 제일 먼저 하나만 고르면요?" (PG narrow "well", Shape Up narrowing)
   - Strong emotion or a story → "그때 무엇이 제일 불편했어요?" (JTBD push, Migicovsky "hardest part", Secondary)
   - No more than two follow-ups, then move on (OARS "don't ask three questions in a row").
5. **"모르겠어요" is a first-class answer.** It is stored as an assumption with evidence "none" and a draft research task ("경쟁 서비스 조사", "고객 5명 인터뷰"). Dolnicar and Grun (Secondary) support offering it, because people mostly use it when they genuinely cannot answer. To guard against satisficing (Krosnick, Secondary), ask one "짐작이라도 괜찮아요. 굳이 고른다면?" before accepting it on required questions. The guess is stored with evidence "guess", not as fact.
6. **Playback after each group**, in the owner's own words: "내가 이해한 건 이거예요. [2 to 3 lines]. 맞나요? 고칠 곳이 있으면 말해 주세요." (practitioner playback, MI summaries at transitions, "reflect the client's words"). Mark each line as 오너가 말함, 제가 추측함, or 아직 모름, which is the console's "confidence of every claim visible" rule from CLAUDE.md.
7. **Length.** Aim for about 10 to 12 minutes and about 15 required questions in the first sitting (Qualtrics 12-minute and SurveyMonkey 15-question drop-off, both survey data). Show progress by group ("7개 중 3번째"), not by question count. Allow "여기까지 하고 나중에 계속" at any group boundary, and resume where it stopped.
8. **Skip rules.** G0, G1 Q1, G2 Q1, G4 Q1 and Q4, G5 Q1, and G7 Q1 are required, since the roadmap cannot be gated without them. Everything else can be "나중에", and the CEO fills a default and marks it 추측. A skipped required question blocks only the stage that needs it, not the whole roadmap.
9. **Closing playback.** Show the whole brief, ask "제일 틀렸을 것 같은 곳이 어디예요?", then confirm. Recap within the session instead of "24 hours" (practitioner guide), because the brief is the written summary.
10. **What not to do.** Don't pitch the CEO's own idea of the solution during the interview (Mom Test "talk around your idea"). Don't ask "좋은 아이디어 같아요?". Don't ask more than three open-text questions in a row without a chip-style question between them (Qualtrics, Secondary).

Mapping to the roadmap (design inference):
- **Stage 0, 알아보기.** One task per important, no-evidence assumption (Strategyzer top-right quadrant), plus every "모르겠어요". Gate: the top three assumptions have at least "들었음" evidence.
- **Stage 1, 문제 확인.** Talk to the G2 people. Gate evidence is a Mom Test commitment (time, reputation, money), not a compliment.
- **Stage 2, 작게 만들기.** Solution sized to the G4 appetite, with No-gos and premortem rabbit holes written into the plan (Shape Up). Gate: the G0 Q2 experience works for one real person.
- **Stage 3, 내놓기 또는 팔아보기.** Altman's "launch it or try to sell it". Gate: the G5 success number, or the stop line.

### B4. Lighter re-run at each stage gate

About 5 questions and 3 to 5 minutes. Show the current brief first, then ask only about the difference.

1. "이번 단계에서 새로 알게 된 것 중 제일 놀라운 건요?" The CEO drafts it from stage results and the owner confirms. (YC "did anything change?", "what did you learn")
2. "예산, 기간, 절대 안 할 일 중 바뀐 게 있어요?" (G4 recheck)
3. "성공 숫자는 그대로 괜찮아요? 그만두는 조건은요?" (Cagan go or no-go, Seibel "where do you want to be week over week")
4. "지금 제일 걱정되는 건요?" This is a two-minute premortem refresh, and the CEO shows the top remaining assumption from the map. (Klein, Strategyzer)
5. "직접 정하고 싶은 것이 바뀌었어요?" (DACI)

Then a playback of the brief diff ("바뀐 곳은 이 세 줄이에요. 맞나요?"), and the brief gets a new version so the history of why the plan changed is kept (Atlassian "Revisit the plans and project poster as the project progresses", DACI "document the final decision"). If the stage disproved a core assumption in G2 or G5, the CEO offers the full G1 to G2 interview again in place of the light version (YC pivot question).
